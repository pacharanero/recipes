# Water-Repellent Wool Dressing (Lanolin Treatments)

Use these small-scale treatments to give felted wool a light water-repellent finish without an oily surface. Test every treatment on a spare offcut before using it on finished work.

## Ingredients

### Lanolin-Only Bath (Makes 1 L)

- 10 g anhydrous pale lanolin
- 3 g Polysorbate 80
- 987 g distilled water

### Lanolin And Beeswax Bath (Makes 1 L)

- 9 g anhydrous pale lanolin
- 1 g pale beeswax
- 5 g Polysorbate 80
- 985 g distilled water

### Alcohol-Carrier Screening Test (Makes 25 g)

- 0.5 g anhydrous pale lanolin
- 24.5 g 99% isopropyl alcohol or ethanol

## Instructions

### Lanolin-Only Bath

1. Pre-wet the wool in warm water and gently squeeze out the excess.
2. Melt the lanolin in a water bath at 45-55 C.
3. Stir the Polysorbate 80 into the melted lanolin.
4. Warm the distilled water to 50-60 C, then whisk in the lanolin mixture until the bath is evenly cloudy.
5. Submerge the wool for 10-20 minutes, gently squeezing it under the surface for even uptake.
6. Remove without rinsing and blot firmly between paper towels until they no longer pick up an oily sheen.
7. Dry flat in a warm, ventilated place for 24-48 hours.

### Lanolin And Beeswax Bath

1. Melt the lanolin and beeswax together in a water bath at the lowest temperature that gives a clear liquid.
2. Stir in the Polysorbate 80, then whisk the mixture into distilled water warmed to 50-60 C.
3. Leave the bath for 10 minutes. Discard it if a surface oil layer forms.
4. Treat, blot and dry the wool as for the lanolin-only bath.

### Alcohol-Carrier Screening Test

1. Stir the lanolin and alcohol at room temperature in a closed glass container, away from ignition sources.
2. If the mixture does not become clear, discard the test. Do not heat it or use it on finished work.
3. If clear, apply a very small amount to a dry offcut with a lint-free cloth.
4. Leave it to evaporate completely in a well-ventilated area before testing.

## Notes

- Do not use beeswax in the alcohol-carrier test.
- Do not use a flame, hotplate or hairdryer until all solvent has evaporated.
- After drying, check for oil transfer with white paper towel, then test a cold water drop and a hot mug.
- If the wool feels oily after drying, reduce the lanolin concentration. If it is dry but water soaks in immediately, repeat the lanolin-only treatment rather than increasing the first-bath concentration.

# Caramelised Onion Chutney

A dark, thick, jammy chutney for a cheeseboard, a ploughman's or a sausage sandwich. The onions are properly caramelised first and only then met with sugar and vinegar, which is what separates a glossy mahogany chutney from the pale, wet, oniony mixture most quick recipes produce. Grated Bramley apple supplies the pectin that gives it a spoonable set. Makes about 1.2 kg, or three to four 340 g jars.

## Ingredients

### Onions

- 1.5 kg red onions, halved and finely sliced 3-5 mm thick
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 1 tsp fine salt

### Sweet And Sour

- 1 Bramley apple (about 200 g), peeled, cored and coarsely grated
- 200 g dark brown soft sugar or light muscovado
- 200 ml red wine vinegar
- 150 ml balsamic vinegar
- 100 ml red wine (optional)

### Aromatics

- 4 sprigs fresh thyme
- 2 bay leaves
- 1 tsp freshly ground black pepper
- 1/2 tsp chilli flakes (optional)
- 1 tsp fine salt, to finish

## Instructions

1. Put the sliced onions, olive oil and 1 tsp salt into a wide heavy-bottomed pan. Cover and cook over a low heat for 15 minutes, stirring once or twice. The salt draws the water out of the onions and they will collapse into their own liquid.
2. Uncover, raise the heat slightly to medium-low, and cook for 45 to 60 minutes, stirring every 5 minutes or so and scraping the base. The onions will go from pale, to golden, to a deep mahogany brown, and will reduce to roughly a third of their starting volume. Do not rush this stage and do not let it catch.
3. Whenever a brown crust builds on the base of the pan, add a splash of water and scrape it up before it burns. Repeat as often as needed - that crust is where most of the flavour is.
4. Stir in the grated apple, sugar, red wine vinegar, balsamic vinegar and red wine, then add the thyme, bay leaves, black pepper and chilli flakes if using. Stir until the sugar has completely dissolved.
5. Bring to a gentle simmer and cook uncovered for 40 to 50 minutes, stirring regularly and scraping the base, until dark, glossy and thick.
6. Test for the set: draw a wooden spoon across the base of the pan. The channel should hold open for 2 to 3 seconds and no thin vinegar should run back into it. If liquid floods the channel, keep reducing.
7. Fish out and discard the bay leaves and the thyme stalks. Stir in the final 1 tsp salt, then taste and adjust with a little more sugar for roundness or vinegar for sharpness.
8. Spoon the chutney boiling hot into hot sterilised jars, filling to within 1 cm of the rim and tapping out air pockets. Seal immediately with vinegar-proof lids.
9. Label and leave to mature in a cool, dark place for at least 4 weeks before opening.

## Storage

Stored sealed in a cool, dark place, the chutney keeps for up to a year and improves for the first two months as the vinegar mellows. Refrigerate after opening and use within 4 weeks.

Use lids with an intact plastic or lacquered coating. Bare metal corrodes on contact with vinegar fumes, and wax discs are destroyed by them, so neither is suitable for chutney.

Onions are a low-acid vegetable at around pH 5.3 to 5.8 and they buffer added acid strongly, so the vinegar here is doing preservation work as well as flavour work. Do not reduce it. If you want to verify shelf stability rather than rely on the recipe, check the cooled chutney with a calibrated pH meter and treat only a reading of 4.2 or lower as safe for ambient storage; otherwise keep the jars in the fridge and use within 2 months. Discard any jar that bulges, smells yeasty, or shows mould.

## Variations

### Sweet White Onion And Cider

Paler, softer and sweeter, and better with a strong blue cheese. Replace the red onions with the same weight of white or yellow onions, the red wine vinegar with cider vinegar, the balsamic with a further 100 ml cider vinegar plus 2 tbsp black treacle for colour, and the red wine with dry cider. Reduce the thyme to 2 sprigs and add 1 tsp yellow mustard seeds with the sugar.

### Rich Onion Jam (Fridge Only)

A dessert-sweet, buttery version for burgers and steak sandwiches, not a keeping preserve. Use butter in place of the olive oil, halve the vinegars to 100 ml red wine vinegar and 75 ml balsamic, omit the apple, and finish with 1 tbsp brandy. There is nowhere near enough acid here to keep at room temperature. Refrigerate in a covered container and use within 2 weeks, or freeze in small portions for up to 3 months.

## Notes

- Add the sugar and vinegar only after the onions have browned. Acid slows the Maillard browning that gives the chutney its colour and savoury depth, and sugar added early will scorch long before the onions are ready.
- Forty-five minutes to an hour of caramelising is the real figure. Recipes promising caramelised onions in 10 or 15 minutes are describing softened, lightly browned onions, and a chutney built on those tastes raw and sharp however long you then simmer it.
- Do not use bicarbonate of soda to speed up browning. It works, but it turns the onions to mush and leaves a soapy note that the vinegar will not cover.
- Slice by hand rather than in a food processor. Processor-cut onions shed too much water and break down into a purée instead of holding as distinct strands in the finished chutney.
- The Bramley is there for pectin and body rather than flavour. A firm eating apple will not set the chutney in the same way. If you have none, reduce for a further 10 to 15 minutes instead.
- The chutney thickens noticeably as it cools, so stop while it is still a little looser than you want it.
- A tablespoon of the wholegrain mustard from this collection stirred in at the end of step 7 makes a sharper, more textured version for cold meats.

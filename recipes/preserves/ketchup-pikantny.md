# Ketchup Pikantny (Polish-Style Spiced Tomato Ketchup)

A smooth, glossy, pourable ketchup built on the Heinz balance of sweet, sharp and tomato-forward, then pushed towards the Polish shelf: celery and onion purée for savoury depth, a warm chilli lift, and a background sourness from fermented dill pickle brine. It is the spicing of the Tomato Relish in this collection - allspice, celery, mustard - taken through a sieve and reduced until it clings to a chip. Makes about 1.3 litres.

## Ingredients

### Base

- 2.5 kg ripe tomatoes, roughly chopped (plum or Roma for the best flesh-to-water ratio)
- 200 g tomato purée (double concentrate)
- 250 g onion, roughly chopped
- 150 g celery sticks or peeled celeriac, roughly chopped
- 150 g red pepper, seeded and roughly chopped
- 4 garlic cloves, peeled

### Spice Bag

- 2 tsp whole allspice berries
- 6 whole cloves
- 5 cm piece cinnamon stick
- 1 tsp yellow mustard seeds
- 1 tsp celery seeds
- 1 tsp dill seeds
- 1 tsp black peppercorns
- 1 blade mace, or 1/2 tsp ground mace
- 2 bay leaves
- 1 tsp dried marjoram

### Sweet And Sour

- 220 g caster sugar
- 200 ml spirit vinegar (distilled white)
- 20 g fine salt

### Pikantny Heat

- 2 long red chillies, stalks removed, roughly chopped
- 2 tsp sweet paprika
- 1/2 tsp cayenne pepper, plus more to taste

### Finishing

- 100 ml brine from fermented dill pickles (ogórki kiszone)
- 1 tbsp finely grated fresh horseradish
- 15 g fresh dill, stalks and all
- 15 g cornflour mixed with 60 ml cold water (optional, for extra cling)

## Instructions

1. Put the tomatoes, onion, celery, red pepper, garlic and chillies into a wide heavy-bottomed pan. Cover and cook over a low heat for 45 minutes, stirring occasionally, until everything has collapsed and released its liquid. Add no water and no oil.
2. Tie the allspice, cloves, cinnamon, mustard seeds, celery seeds, dill seeds, peppercorns, mace, bay leaves and marjoram into a square of muslin.
3. Stir in the tomato purée, sugar, spirit vinegar and salt, and add the spice bag. Simmer uncovered for 30 minutes, stirring regularly and scraping the base, so the spices infuse and the sugar dissolves completely.
4. Remove and discard the spice bag, pressing it against the side of the pan to release the liquid it holds.
5. Blitz the mixture to a purée with a stick blender, then pass it through a fine sieve or a food mill fitted with a fine screen, working it through with a ladle. Discard the skins and seeds left behind. Rinse the sieve and pass the purée through a second time - this second pass is what separates a ketchup from a chutney.
6. Return the purée to the cleaned pan, stir in the paprika and cayenne, and simmer uncovered over a low heat for 1 to 2 hours, stirring every few minutes, until reduced to about 1.3 litres. It should mound briefly on a spoon and a line drawn through it on a cold plate should hold for a second before closing.
7. If using the cornflour, stir the slurry into the simmering ketchup, return it to a full boil and cook for 1 to 2 minutes, stirring, until glossy.
8. Take the pan off the heat. Stir in the pickle brine and the grated horseradish, then submerge the fresh dill in the hot ketchup, cover and leave to infuse for 20 minutes.
9. Lift out and discard the dill, then pass the ketchup through the fine sieve a final time so no fibres remain.
10. Taste and adjust: more salt for savouriness, more vinegar for sharpness, more cayenne for heat, more sugar to round it out. Reheat to a simmer.
11. Funnel the hot ketchup into clean, sterilised bottles, leaving 1 cm headspace. Leave uncovered until completely cool, then seal and refrigerate.

## Storage

Keep refrigerated and use within 3 months. Discard any bottle that smells fermented, fizzes on opening, or shows mould.

For ambient storage the ketchup must be measurably acidic. Check the cooled sauce with a calibrated pH meter and only water-bath process it if it reads pH 4.2 or lower; then fill sterilised jars or bottles to 1 cm headspace, seal, and process for 15 minutes in boiling water, adding time for altitude. Do not rely on the pickle brine for acidity - its strength varies from jar to jar and it is added for flavour, not preservation.

## Notes

- The celery is not optional. Polish ketchups such as Włocławek are built on celery and onion purée, and UK Heinz gets a version of the same note from a celery-containing spice extract. It is the savoury floor the whole sauce stands on. Celeriac gives a rounder, sweeter result than celery sticks.
- Clove, allspice and cinnamon in that order of prominence are what make a sauce read as ketchup rather than as tomato sauce. Keep them in a bag so they can come out before the flavour turns medicinal.
- At 220 g sugar and 20 g salt the finished ketchup lands at roughly 15 g sugar and 1.3 g salt per 100 g, noticeably less sweet and less salty than the commercial article. For a shop-accurate profile, increase the sugar to 320 g and the salt to 27 g.
- Fresh dill is added off the heat and strained out because boiling strips it. Dill seeds in the spice bag carry the same note through the long simmer, so both are needed.
- For a more pickle-forward sauce, blitz 50 g drained fermented dill pickle into the purée before the final sieving, and increase the brine to 150 ml.
- Horseradish fades within a fortnight in the fridge. If it matters, grate a little fresh into the bottle you are using rather than into the whole batch.
- Tomato variety drives the reduction time far more than the recipe does. Watery salad tomatoes can take an extra hour; if you are short of patience, increase the tomato purée to 300 g and start with 2 kg of fresh tomatoes.
- For a milder ketchup for children, omit the fresh chillies and cayenne and keep the paprika. The celery, dill and allspice character survives intact.

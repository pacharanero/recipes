# Sweet Baby Ray's-Style BBQ Sauce (Clone)

A thick, glossy, very sweet Kansas City-style barbecue sauce with a sharp vinegar tang, a hickory smoke note and a fruity pineapple-tamarind background. Makes about 2 litres.

## Ingredients

### Base

- 500 ml water
- 400 g tomato purée (double concentrate)
- 700 g golden syrup
- 350 g light brown soft sugar
- 50 g black treacle
- 400 ml distilled white vinegar
- 100 ml cider vinegar
- 200 ml pineapple juice
- 45 ml Worcestershire sauce (3 tbsp)
- 30 g tamarind paste (2 tbsp)

### Seasoning

- 30 g fine salt
- 3 tbsp garlic powder
- 2 tbsp onion powder
- 2 tbsp English mustard powder
- 2 tbsp sweet smoked paprika
- 2 tsp freshly ground black pepper
- 1 tsp cayenne pepper
- 15 ml hickory liquid smoke (1 tbsp)

### Thickening

- 45 g cornflour
- 120 ml cold water

### Fresh Apricot-Tamarind Variation

Use this variation in place of the indicated base ingredients. Keep all other ingredients and quantities the same.

- 250 g tomato purée plus 50 g sun-dried tomato purée, instead of 400 g tomato purée
- 568 ml distilled white vinegar, instead of the white and cider vinegars
- 100 g tamarind paste, instead of 30 g
- 2 large fresh tomatoes, roughly chopped
- 4 fresh apricots, stoned and roughly chopped
- 1 whole head of garlic, cloves separated and peeled, instead of garlic powder
- 1 whole onion, roughly chopped, instead of onion powder
- 1 small piece fresh ginger (about 10 g), peeled and roughly chopped
- 30 g capers
- 10 g yellow mustard seeds
- juice of 1 lime

## Instructions

1. Pour 2 litres of water into the pan you intend to use, stand a wooden skewer upright in it and mark the water line, then tip the water away and dry the pan. Use this mark to judge the reduction later.
2. If using the fresh apricot-tamarind variation, blitz the tomatoes, apricots, garlic, onion, ginger, capers, mustard seeds and sun-dried tomato purée to a smooth puree. Add this to the pan with the water and tomato purée.
3. Whisk the water and tomato purée together in the pan until smooth, then add the golden syrup, brown sugar, black treacle, vinegar, pineapple juice, Worcestershire sauce and tamarind paste. Add the lime juice now if using the variation.
4. Add the salt, garlic powder, onion powder, mustard powder, smoked paprika, black pepper and cayenne. Whisk thoroughly, breaking up any lumps of powder against the side of the pan. Omit the garlic powder and onion powder when using the variation.
5. Bring to a gentle simmer over a medium heat, stirring constantly until the sugars have fully dissolved and nothing is sticking to the base.
6. Reduce the heat to low and simmer uncovered for 30-40 minutes, stirring every few minutes and scraping the base, until reduced to about 1.85 litres - roughly 1 cm below the skewer mark. The sauce will darken and thicken slightly.
7. Stir in the liquid smoke.
8. Mix the cornflour with the cold water to a smooth slurry. Stir it into the sauce, bring back to a full boil and cook for 1-2 minutes, stirring, until glossy and thick enough to coat the back of a spoon. It should now sit at the 2 litre mark.
9. Taste and adjust: more salt for savouriness, more vinegar for tang, more cayenne for heat. Remove from the heat.
10. Pour or funnel the hot sauce into clean, sterilised bottles or jars, leaving 1 cm headspace. Leave uncovered until completely cool, then seal and refrigerate.

## Storage

Keep refrigerated and use within 3 months. The high sugar and vinegar content preserves the sauce, but it contains no added preservatives, so it is intended for refrigerated storage rather than the ambient pantry. Discard any bottle that smells fermented or shows mould.

## Notes

- The sauce thickens noticeably as it cools, so stop while it still pours easily.
- Golden syrup stands in for the corn syrup in the original and is what gives the sauce its clinging, non-grainy body. Do not replace it with more granulated sugar.
- For a label-accurate saltiness, increase the salt to 45 g. At 30 g the sauce reads sweeter and less salty, which suits most British palates.
- All 500 ml of vinegar can be distilled white if preferred; the cider vinegar simply adds a little depth.
- Liquid smoke is essential to the flavour. If it is unavailable, increase the smoked paprika to 3 tbsp, but the result will be milder and earthier.
- For a smaller batch, divide every quantity by four to make about 500 ml, and reduce the simmering time to 12-15 minutes.

# No-Churn Chocolate-Banana Ice Cream

Single-batch no-churn ice cream, adapted from a two-way (chocolate-banana and vanilla) recipe so it makes one tub of chocolate-banana only. Homemade condensed milk folded into whipped double cream; no machine needed. Makes about 1 litre.

## Ingredients

### Condensed milk base

- 800 ml whole milk
- 160 g caster sugar
- a pinch of salt (about 1/8 tsp)

### Flavouring

- 2 tsp vanilla extract
- 4 tbsp cacao powder
- 3 very ripe bananas

### To fold

- 1 litre double cream, well chilled

## Instructions

1. **Reduce the milk.** Put the milk, caster sugar and salt in your widest pan. Bring to a simmer, then keep it at a gentle bubble, stirring every few minutes and scraping the base, until reduced to roughly 375 ml. It should be thick, glossy and lightly golden. A wide pan makes this much quicker.
2. **Flavour.** Stir the vanilla extract into the warm condensed milk, then whisk in the cacao powder while it is still warm so it dissolves without lumps.
3. **Cool completely.** Chill in the fridge until cold. Warm condensed milk will deflate the cream.
4. **Add the banana.** Mash the bananas until completely smooth and stir into the condensed milk.
5. **Whip the cream.** Whip the chilled double cream to soft peaks, where it holds its shape but still slumps a little. Stop before it goes stiff or grainy.
6. **Fold.** Fold the whipped cream into the banana mixture a third at a time, keeping as much air in as you can.
7. **Freeze.** Scrape into a lidded tub, press baking paper onto the surface, and freeze until firm. No stirring needed.

## Notes

- Taste before folding: if the bananas were very sweet you won't need anything else; if not, a spoonful of honey helps.
- The banana adds water so the finished ice cream is slightly firmer; give it 10 minutes out of the freezer before scooping.
- Shortcut: a 397 g tin of condensed milk replaces steps 1-2 if you don't want to stand over a pan.

### Chocolate cake mix-in (Phish Food style)

Crumbling in small pieces of frozen chocolate cake gives a Ben & Jerry's-style texture. This is how commercial ice creams get their chunky swirls, and the frozen cake keeps its bite instead of going soggy.

- Cut the cake into small (1-2 cm) cubes or coarse crumbs while it is frozen solid. Slightly thawed cake smears into streaks rather than staying as chunks. Firm sponge and ganache or icing both hold up well; soft fresh cream fillings go icy.
- Fold the cake pieces in at step 7, as you scrape the ice cream into the tub. Do not blend them in, or you will knock out the air you just folded in. Layering works well: half the ice cream, a sprinkle of cake, the rest of the ice cream.
- Keep the ratio modest: roughly 100-150 g of cake per litre of ice cream. Cake is sugary and low in water, so in moderation the pieces stay chewy-fudgy; too much makes the whole tub scoop firm and slightly crumbly.
- If the cake has a buttercream or ganache filling, expect those pieces to be the fudgiest bits.

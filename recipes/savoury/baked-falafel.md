# Baked Falafel

An Egyptian family recipe adapted for the oven. Crispy on the outside, fluffy inside, and full of fresh herbs. Makes around 24 falafel; serves 8 as a side or 4 as a main.

> **Note on chickpea prep:** This recipe requires soaked-but-raw dried chickpeas - not cooked ones. Fully cooking the chickpeas (e.g. in a pressure cooker) results in a texture similar to canned chickpeas, which tends to make falafel dense and crumbly. The overnight cold soak is what gives authentic falafel its light, airy texture. Plan ahead - the chickpeas need at least 18 hours in water.

## Ingredients

- 400g (2 cups) dried chickpeas
- ½ tsp bicarbonate of soda (for soaking)
- 1 tsp chickpea (gram) flour
- 1 tsp baking powder
- 30g (1 cup) fresh flat-leaf parsley leaves
- 20g (¾ cup) fresh coriander leaves
- 15g (½ cup) fresh dill, tough stems removed
- 1 small yellow onion, quartered
- 7-8 garlic cloves, peeled
- 1 tbsp ground black pepper
- 1 tbsp ground cumin
- 1 tbsp ground coriander
- 1 tsp cayenne pepper (optional)
- salt, to taste
- 2 tbsp sesame seeds, toasted
- extra virgin olive oil, for coating

## Instructions

### The day before: soak the chickpeas

1. Place the dried chickpeas and bicarbonate of soda in a large bowl. Cover with at least 5cm of cold water. Soak overnight for at least 18 hours. They are ready when a chickpea easily falls apart when squeezed between your fingers.

### Make the falafel mixture (1 hour before baking)

2. Drain the soaked chickpeas thoroughly and pat dry with a clean tea towel - removing excess moisture is important.

3. Transfer to a food processor fitted with an S-blade. Add the chickpea flour, baking powder, parsley, coriander leaves, dill, onion, and garlic. Season with the black pepper, ground cumin, ground coriander, cayenne (if using), and a generous pinch of salt.

4. Pulse in 40-second bursts until everything is well combined into a coarse, green-flecked mixture. It should hold together when pressed but not be smooth or paste-like.

5. Transfer to a sealed container and refrigerate for at least 1 hour (or up to 24 hours). Do not skip this step - chilling is what holds them together without egg or extra flour.

### Bake

6. Preheat the oven to 180C/160C fan/gas 4 (350°F). Lightly oil a large baking sheet.

7. Stir the toasted sesame seeds into the chilled mixture.

8. With damp hands, shape the mixture into balls or patties about 4cm across (roughly 1 heaped tablespoon each). Place on the oiled baking sheet and brush the tops with olive oil.

9. Bake for 8 minutes. Flip each one, then bake for a further 7-12 minutes until golden brown and cooked through.

10. Rest for a few minutes before serving.

## Notes

- If you cannot find chickpea (gram) flour, plain flour works as a substitute for the small quantity used here.
- To freeze: arrange unbaked falafel on a lined tray, freeze until solid, then transfer to a freezer bag. They keep for up to 1 month. Bake from frozen, adding around 5 minutes to the cook time.
- If the mixture is too wet to shape, refrigerate for longer or stir in a little extra chickpea flour.

## Serving suggestions

Serve in warm pitta with tahini sauce, sliced cucumber and tomato, baby rocket, and pickled turnips or red onions. Or serve as part of a mezze spread with hummus and a green salad.

# Overnight Pizza Dough

A slow-cold-fermented pizza dough based on Peter Reinhart's Pizza Napoletana. The overnight rest in the fridge develops flavour and produces a lighter, more complex crust than a same-day dough. Requires making the dough the day before baking. Makes 4 pizzas.

## Ingredients

- 600g unbleached strong white bread flour, plus extra for dusting
- 10g salt (1¾ tsp)
- 1 tsp instant dried yeast
- 60ml olive oil
- 425ml ice-cold water (around 40°F/4°C)

## Instructions

### The day before

1. **Mix the dough.** Stir together the flour, salt and yeast in a large bowl (or the bowl of a stand mixer). Stir in the olive oil and ice-cold water until all the flour is absorbed.

2. **Knead.** If mixing by hand, repeatedly dip one hand in cold water and use it like a dough hook to work the dough vigorously into a smooth mass while rotating the bowl with the other hand. Reverse the circular motion a few times to develop the gluten. Do this for 5-7 minutes. If using a stand mixer, switch to the dough hook and mix on medium speed for 5-7 minutes until smooth and sticky - the dough should clear the sides of the bowl but stick to the bottom. Adjust with a little more flour or water if needed. The finished dough will be springy, elastic and sticky, and register 10-13°C.

3. **Divide and shape.** Sprinkle flour on the counter and transfer the dough. Using a dough scraper or sharp knife, cut the dough into 4 equal pieces (press down to pinch off, don't saw). Flour your hands and gently round each piece into a ball.

4. **Refrigerate overnight.** Transfer the dough balls to a lined tray, mist generously with oil, and cover tightly with cling film or place in a food-grade plastic bag. Refrigerate overnight, or up to 3 days.

### On the day

5. **Bring to room temperature.** Remove the dough balls from the fridge 2 hours before baking. Dust the counter with flour, place the balls on top, and dust them with flour. Gently press each into a disc about 1cm thick and 12cm across. Sprinkle with flour, mist with oil, cover loosely with cling film or a tea towel, and rest for 2 hours (longer in a cold kitchen).

6. **Preheat the oven.** At least 45 minutes before baking, place a pizza stone or upturned baking tray on a rack in the lower third of the oven. Heat the oven as hot as it will go - up to 260°C/500°F or higher if your oven allows.

7. **Shape the pizzas.** Generously dust a peel or the back of a baking sheet with flour (or use parchment paper). With floured hands, lift one dough ball and gently stretch it across your knuckles, bouncing in a circular motion and giving a little stretch each time. Keep stretching until 23-30cm across. Lay on the peel. Don't use a rolling pin if you can help it - it compresses the bubbles and makes the crust denser.

8. **Top and bake.** Brush the edge with a little olive oil, add toppings (less is more - a few toppings keeps the crust crisp), and slide the pizza onto the hot stone. Bake for 5-8 minutes, rotating after 2 minutes if needed for even baking. Transfer to a board, rest 3-5 minutes, then slice. Repeat with the remaining dough.

## Notes

- Cold water is critical - it keeps the dough from warming during kneading, which would make it sticky and hard to handle.
- To freeze: dip each dough ball in oil, place in separate freezer bags, and freeze for up to 3 months. Transfer to the fridge the day before you plan to bake.
- If the bottom crisps before the cheese caramelises, move the stone to a higher shelf. If the top gets done before the bottom, move it lower.
- For best results keep toppings light (3-4 max including sauce and cheese), and drain wet ingredients like fresh mozzarella to avoid a soggy base.

# Simple Pale Ale

_Bottled as "Deranged Penguin"_

A deliberately plain single-malt, single-hop pale ale brewed on 7 June 2025 on the usual 30 L single-vessel setup. One bag of pale malt, a generous charge of Hallertau Mittelfrüh in a short boil, and a lager yeast fermented at household temperature for a clean finish.

- Batch size: approximately 20 L
- Original gravity: approximately 1.050-1.055 (estimated; not measured on the day)
- Final gravity: approximately 1.010-1.012
- ABV: approximately 5.0-5.5%
- Colour: pale gold

## Ingredients

- Approximately 20 L brewing water
- 5 kg crushed pale malt
- 100 g Hallertau Mittelfrüh hops
- 1 pack Einstein lager yeast
- Approximately 110 g white table sugar for priming 20 L at 20°C to about 2.3 volumes of CO2; recalculate for the actual packaged volume and highest post-fermentation temperature

## Instructions

1. Heat approximately 20 L of water to boiling, then cool it to 66°C.
2. Add the crushed pale malt and mash at 66°C for 60 minutes.
3. Run the wort into a clean fermentation vessel, sparging as the vessel capacity allows.
4. Remove the spent grain, rinse the stockpot, return the wort and bring it to a rolling boil.
5. Boil for 30 minutes, adding the whole 100 g of Hallertau Mittelfrüh at the start of the boil.
6. Cool the wort promptly to 18°C and transfer it to the clean fermentation vessel.
7. Pitch the Einstein lager yeast the following day once the wort has settled at pitching temperature.
8. Ferment at ambient household temperature until fermentation is complete, confirmed by stable hydrometer readings over two or three days.
9. Dissolve the priming sugar in a small reserved quantity of beer using sanitised equipment, then divide the mixture evenly between clean pressure-rated bottles as the beer is filled.
10. Seal, label and condition in protected storage boxes for at least two weeks.

## Notes

- The original brewing note recorded only the water volume, malt, hop charge, "short boil" and the yeast pitched on 8 June 2025. The mash temperature, 30 minute boil length and packaging steps follow the usual house method.
- A short boil keeps the beer pale and preserves more of the noble hop aroma, but extracts less bitterness, so the full 100 g charge gives a moderate rather than sharp bitterness.
- Fermenting a lager yeast at ambient household temperature produces a clean, lightly fruity pale ale rather than a true cold-conditioned lager.
- Do not bottle on elapsed time alone. Confirm fermentation is complete first, and measure a calculated total priming sugar mass for the actual finished volume.
